# IdleCommand — Design Authority

This document is the project's design constitution. It converts the Game Design Bible into enforceable decision rules.

## Authority order

When documents, prompts, prototypes, code, or ideas conflict, use this order:

1. `docs/foundation/DESIGN_AUTHORITY.md`
2. `docs/foundation/GAME_DESIGN_BIBLE.md`
3. Approved milestone documents in `roadmap/`
4. Technical plans and implementation notes
5. Individual feature ideas

A feature is not approved merely because it is technically impressive or entertaining in isolation.

## Non-negotiable rules

### 1. Attention must be earned

IdleCommand must never rely on urgency, red dots, streaks, countdown pressure, repeated alerts, or fear of missing out.

### 2. Absence must be safe

The player may ignore the world for hours, days, or longer without punishment, irreversible neglect, or guilt messaging.

### 3. Observation comes before control

Characters remain autonomous. Direct commands, job assignment, and manual unit control are outside the core experience.

### 4. No permanent management HUD

Desktop mode must not become a dashboard of resources, bars, counters, tasks, XP, or production metrics.

### 5. Show causality in the world

Whenever practical, represent state through animation, environment, props, routines, and consequences rather than labels and percentages.

### 6. Stories emerge from systems

Prefer connected rules involving traits, memories, relationships, weather, needs, place, and time over fixed quest chains.

### 7. Characters are inhabitants, not resources

People and animals are not interchangeable production units. Their individuality and autonomy are essential.

### 8. Calm is functional

Performance, motion, sound, timing, density, and interaction must support long-running calm desktop presence.

### 9. History must leave traces

Development should accumulate visible and recorded history rather than repeatedly wiping the world clean.

### 10. Camp Age is the only active age

No later era may expand production scope until Milestone 0.0.1 proves that the tiny camp is compelling on its own.

### 11. Build the simulation before the spectacle

Placeholder visuals are acceptable during systems testing. A beautiful but lifeless world does not pass.

### 12. Fewer, deeper behaviours

Prefer five behaviours with context, variation, and emotional readability over fifty shallow animations.

### 13. Desktop mode is the primary product

A larger world view supports inspection, but it must not quietly replace the desktop-companion experience with a conventional full-screen game.

### 14. No monetised impatience

The vision is incompatible with paid speed-ups, energy systems, manipulative waiting mechanics, or progression pressure.

### 15. Every feature must pass the glance test

A feature should improve what the player can notice, understand, feel, or remember during a brief glance.

## Feature approval test

Before implementation, answer:

1. Does this make the world feel more alive?
2. Can the feature be understood without a permanent HUD?
3. Does it preserve character autonomy?
4. Can it be safely ignored?
5. Does it create observable consequences or meaningful history?
6. Does it support calm rather than demand attention?
7. Is it necessary for the current milestone?

A feature that fails questions 2, 3, 4, or 6 should normally be rejected or redesigned.

## Red flags

Stop and review when a proposal includes phrases such as:

- daily reward
- claim now
- energy refill
- production bonus
- resource counter
- optimise workers
- assign villager
- upgrade every building
- limited-time event
- login streak
- push notification
- collect all

These concepts are not automatically forbidden in every possible form, but they strongly indicate movement away from the product identity.

## Exception process

An exception requires an explicit written amendment explaining:

- why the rule is insufficient
- what player experience improves
- how the change avoids attention manipulation
- what simpler alternative was considered

Until such an amendment is accepted, the existing authority remains binding.
